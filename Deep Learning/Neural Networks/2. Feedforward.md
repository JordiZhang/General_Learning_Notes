Let each of the inputs be $x_i$ and each of the neuron outputs in the first hidden layer be $z_j$. Consider a neural network with $K$ inputs and $M$ neurons in the single hidden layer fed into a single output neuron. We denote the activation function of the hidden layer as $a_{1}$ and the activation function of the output as $a_2$. Each neuron applies a linear transformation to the input vector and then applies the activation function:
$$z_{j}=a_{1}\left( \sum_{i=1}^K(W_{ji}x_{i})+b_{j} \right)$$
where $W_{ij}$ is the weight corresponding to neuron $i$ and input $j$ and is thus a matrix of size $\text{Neurons in layer} \times \text{Inputs}$.
$$
Z=
\begin{bmatrix}
z_{1}\\
\vdots \\
z_{M}
\end{bmatrix}
=a_{1} \left(
\begin{bmatrix}
\sum_{i=1}^K(W_{1i}x_{i})+b_{1} \\
\vdots \\
\sum_{i=1}^K(W_{Mi}x_{i})+b_{M}
\end{bmatrix}
\right)
=
a_{1}
\left(
\begin{bmatrix}
W_{11} & \cdots & W_{1K} \\
\vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
W_{M1} & \cdots & W_{MK}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
x_{1} \\
\vdots \\
x_{K}
\end{bmatrix}
+
\begin{bmatrix}
b_{1} \\
\vdots \\
b_{M}
\end{bmatrix}
\right)
$$
Simplifying into matrices and vectors we get:
$$
Z=a_{1}(Wx+b)
$$
Now we can calculate the output:
$$
y=a_{2}(Va_{1}(Wx+b)+c)
$$
where $V$ is the weights matrix of the next layer, in our case the output so it really is just a vector in this case.

In this above working, $x$ is a vector and $x_i$ are components of the vector, however in the following section we will see how to generalize this to any number of samples by considering a matrix $X$ consisting of different samples of $x$. So we will change the notation such that $x_i$ denotes a vector representing sample $i$. Then if we have $N$ samples, we have:
$$
Y=
\begin{bmatrix}
y_{1} &
\cdots &
y_{N}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
a_{2}(Va_{1}(Wx_{1}+b)+c) &
\cdots &
a_{2}(Va_{1}(Wx_{N}+b)+c)
\end{bmatrix}
$$
Then $X =\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} & \cdots & x_{N}\end{bmatrix}$. Note that in implementations, it is usual to use row notation instead, so this would change our expressions.
$$
Y=a_{2}(Va_{1}(WX+b)+c)
$$
Thus we obtain a matrix expression for calculating the output efficiently, this can be extended to any number of layers in the same fashion. One caveat though, $WX$ results in a $M\times N$ matrix while the biases are $M$ length vectors, this is clearly wrong. In theory the biases are also $M\times N$ matrices:
$$
b=\begin{bmatrix}
b&
\cdots &
b
\end{bmatrix}
$$
In practice, NumPy will broadcast them correctly without needing to explicitly construct the bias matrix.

What if we have multiple outputs and we use Softmax as the activation? Then our output is an $K\times N$ matrix. Neurons output probabilities, so in this case, whichever probability is largest, then we classify as that class.
