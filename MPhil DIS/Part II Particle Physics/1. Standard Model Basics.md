## Matter
`Leptons`: Spin $1/2$ fermions with 6 distinct flavours.
`Quarks`: Also spin $1/2$ fermions and 6 distinct flavours. They have fractional charges and are confined to hadrons. Additionally they come in 3 "colours" (3 orthogonal colour charges) RGB. Colour is essentially the charge equivalent for the strong force.
`Hadrons`: Two types. 
- Mesons composed of a quark and an anti quark. They are integer spin bosons
- Baryons composed of 3 quarks (antibaryon composed of 3 antiquarks). They are half-integer spin fermions.
## Fundamental Forces
In particle physics, we utilize a quantum mechanical description for forces. Messenger (virtual) particles, which are spin $1$ bosons, are required to propagate forces between particles. More specifically, forces arise through the exchange of a virtual field quanta called gauge bosons. This violates energy/momentum conservation but is permissible for short times under the uncertainty principle.
- Strong force: Holds quarks in bound states (hadrons and nuclei), and only acts on quarks. It is propagated by gluons (which have colour charge, there are 8 of them).
- Weak force: Relevant for radioactivity, fusion and fission. It acts on all particles and is propagated by $W$ and $Z$ bosons.
- Electromagnetic force: Relevant for chemistry and biology. Acts on all charged particles and is propagated by photons.
- Gravity: Weakest force of them all. Mostly irrelevant for subatomic particles. It is not part of the standard model and it is theorized to be propagated by gravitons.
## Symmetries and Conservation Laws
Noether's Theorem states that for every continuous symmetry under a transformation, there is a corresponding conservation law. For example:
- Invariance under translation leads to conservation of momentum.
- Invariance under rotation leads to conservation of angular momentum.
- Invariance under time displacement leads to conservation of energy.
There are also discrete symmetries not covered by Noether's Theorem:
- Invariance under gauge transformations in QED leads to charge conservation.
- Lepton Number ($L_{e}=N(e^-) - N(e^+) + N(\nu_{e})-N(\bar{\nu}_{e})$) is conserved in all known interactions though there is no known underlying symmetry.
- Quark Number ($N_{u}=N(u)-N(\bar{u})$) is conserved in QED and QCD, but not in weak interactions. No known underlying symmetry.
- Baryon Number ($N_{q}=\frac{1}{3}$ and $N_{\bar{q}}=-\frac{1}{3}$) is conserved in all known interactions. No known underlying symmetry. Baryons (made of 3 quarks) have Baryon number $1$, and antibaryons $-1$.
- Parity ($\pm 1$) is conserved in QED and QCD, but not in weak interactions (CP violation). Parity conservation means that physical laws are unchanged under a spatial inversion.
