So far in our discussions of gradient descent we have considered only a fixed learning rate $\eta$. In this section we will discuss how to improve gradient descent by considering momentum and adaptive learning rates. 
# Polyak Momentum
In typical gradient descent we have:
$$
W_{t}= W_{t-1} - \eta\nabla _{W}L_{t}
$$
This however leads to slow learning in shallow areas of the gradient leading to the weights getting stuck in local minima often. Note, $L_{t}=L_{t}(W_{t-1})$ To solve this we add a momentum term:
$$
\begin{align}
W_{t}&= W_{t-1} - v_{t} \\
v_{t}&= \mu v_{t-1} + \eta\nabla _{W}L_{t}
\end{align}
$$
where $\mu$ is the momentum coefficient, typically in the range 0.9-1. In gradient descent, we might imagine taking many small steps towards the minima, momentum accelerates this process by helping the algorithm take larger displacements per step if the gradients continue in the same direction, by accumulating the small displacements. Meanwhile if the gradients are alternating momentum averages them down, thus speeding up training.

There are 2 main conventions for defining gradient descent with momentum, the one above and the one below (TensorFlow):
$$
\begin{align}
W_{t}&= W_{t-1} + v_{t} \\
v_{t}&= \mu v_{t-1} - \eta\nabla _{W}L_{t}
\end{align}
$$
These 2 conventions are equivalent although their expanded expressions differ in the sign of one of the terms. This can be seen by recognizing that there is no $v_0$ so the first velocity $v_1$ is a function of the gradient only and thus the conventions differ only by the sign in the first velocity which gets 'propagated' to the other terms iteratively. This makes sense as the 2 conventions also differ in how we update the weights, one adds while the other subtracts the velocity. 

Note that implementation wise, PyTorch uses a modified version of momentum based gradient descent. Here we also scale the momentum term by the learning rate:
$$
\begin{align}
W_{t}&= W_{t-1} - \eta v_{t} \\
v_{t}&= \mu v_{t-1} + \nabla _{W}L_{t}
\end{align}
$$
This is also equivalent to the other formulations when the learning rate is fixed. 
# Nesterov Momentum
The principle behind Nesterov Momentum is to perform a look-ahead in the weights value to see where it would have landed if we followed the gradient from the previous step scaled by momentum (following the first convention):
$$
W_{t-1}'=W_{t-1}-\mu v_{t-1}
$$
Then we check the gradient at the lookahead value to correct its course from the previous step. 
$$
\begin{align} 
W_{t}&=W_{t-1} - v_{t} \\
v_{t}&=\mu v_{t-1}+\eta \nabla_{W} L_{t}(W_{t-1}') 
\end{align}
$$
Ideally we would like an expression only using $1$ type of $W$, so lets try to rewrite it in terms of $W'$. Then:
$$
\begin{align}
W_{t}&=W_{t-1} - v_{t}  \\
\end{align}
$$
$$
\begin{align}
&\Rightarrow W_{t}'+\mu v_{t}=W_{t-1}'+\mu v_{t-1}-v_{t} \\
&\Rightarrow W_{t}'=W_{t-1}'+\mu v_{t-1}-(\mu+1)v_{t}
\end{align}
$$
Then we substitute for $v_t$:
$$
W_{t}'=W_{t-1}'+\mu v_{t-1} -\mu^2v_{t-1} -\mu\eta \nabla_{W} L_{t}(W_{t-1}') -\mu v_{t-1}-\eta \nabla_{W} L_{t}(W_{t-1}') 
$$
$$
\begin{align}
&\Rightarrow W_{t}'=W_{t-1}'-\mu^2 v_{t-1}-\mu \eta \nabla_{W} L_{t}(W_{t-1}') -\eta \nabla_{W} L_{t}(W_{t-1}')  \\
&\qquad \text{Substitute in for }v_{t} \\
&\Rightarrow W_{t}'=W_{t-1}'-\mu v_{t}-\eta \nabla_{W} L_{t}(W_{t-1}') 
\end{align}
$$
Thus we have shown that by considering look-ahead dynamics we obtain an update rule of the form above, as the update rule is independent of the definition of $W'$. Therefore an update rule of such form is equivalent to look-ahead dynamics, so we can replace the update with our original weights. Thus obtaining Nesterov Momentum:
$$
\begin{align}
W_{t}&=W_{t-1}-\mu v_{t}-\eta \nabla_{W} L_{t}(W_{t-1}) \\
v_{t}&=\mu v_{t-1}+\eta \nabla_{W} L_{t}(W_{t-1}) 
\end{align}
$$
Following the same methodology we can derive the Nesterov Momentum for the other conventions too, TensorFlow:
$$
\begin{align}
W_{t}&=W_{t-1}+\mu v_{t}-\eta \nabla_{W} L_{t}(W_{t-1}) \\
v_{t}&=\mu v_{t-1}-\eta \nabla_{W} L_{t}(W_{t-1}) 
\end{align}
$$
PyTorch:
$$
\begin{align}
W_{t}&=W_{t-1}-\mu \eta v_{t}-\eta \nabla_{W} L_{t}(W_{t-1}) \\
v_{t}&=\mu v_{t-1}+\nabla_{W} L_{t}(W_{t-1}) 
\end{align}
$$
# Alternative Notation
It is common to define 
$$
g_{t}=\nabla_{W} L_{t}
$$
and to use $\theta$ to denote the parameters for the weights instead of $W$.
