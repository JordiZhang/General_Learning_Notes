This is the most commonly used optimizer out of them all and is often the default choice for basically everyone due to its incredibly robust nature even with default settings. This allows us to focus on the rest of the model rather than on another hyperparameter.

Adam is derived from RMSProp, originally devised by Jimmy Ba during his PhD studies while studying under Geoffrey Hinton. It combines ideas from Polyak momentum and RMSProp to built a robust optimizer. It is already possible to use adaptive learning with momentum, however these are not the same as the Adam optimizer as it is simply combining them together. To understand Adam we will first consider the mean of an arbitrary variable.
$$
\bar{X}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N X_{i}
$$
In almost any case, we don't even consider whether this formula is feasible to implement, yet we can see that if $N$ becomes very large, it is not possible to store all the values of $X_i$ in memory to calculate the mean. Every time we get a new value of $X$ we must calculate the mean again from scratch with complexity $O(N)$. We can rearrange the formula in terms of the previous mean to obtain a constant complexity.
$$
\begin{align}
\bar{X}_{N}&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N X_{i}=\frac{1}{N}\left( \sum_{i=1}^{N-1}X_{i}+X_{N} \right) \\
&= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}X_{i}+\frac{1}{N}X_{N} \\
&=\frac{N-1}{N}\bar{X}_{N-1}+\frac{1}{N}X_{N} \\
&=\left( 1-\frac{1}{N} \right)\bar{X}_{N-1}+\frac{1}{N}X_{N}
\end{align}
$$
Now we replace $1/N$ with a constant $\alpha$:
$$
\bar{X}_{N}=(1-\alpha)\bar{X}_{N-1}+\alpha X_{N}
$$
This is the exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) and is no longer the sample mean, instead it estimates the mean by applying weights that decrease exponentially over time, giving higher importance to recent data and less to older data. Conventionally we define it with a decay factor instead as such:
$$
\bar{X}_{N}=\beta\bar{X}_{N-1}+(1-\beta) X_{N}
$$
where the decay factor $\beta=1-\alpha$. Then we recognize that momentum is just utilizing the EWMA of the gradients to optimize training and RMSProp does the same but using the EWMA of the gradients squared. Then combining both ideas gives us Adam as shown below. Notice that these are just estimates of the first and second moments of the gradients $E[g_{t}]$ and $E[g_{t}^2]$, hence the name adaptive moment estimation. We use different notation to better follow the original paper.
$$
\begin{align}
m_{t}&=\beta_{1}m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_{1})g_{t} \\
v_{t}&=\beta_{2}v_{t-1}+(1-\beta_{2})g_{t}^2 \\
W_{t}&=W_{t-1}-\eta\frac{m_{t}}{\sqrt{ v_{t} }+\epsilon}
\end{align}
$$
# Bias Correction
When initializing the EWMAs we usually set the values at $t=0$ to $0$, which introduces initialization bias to the averages. We can think of this as the update rules lagging behind the true moments of the gradients. So we want to adjust the estimators accordingly to correct it. We will show how to do this using the first moments though the same derivation is done for the second ones. First note that we can rewrite the estimate as such:
$$
m_{t}=(1-\beta_{1})\sum_{i=1}^t \beta_{1}^{t-i} g_{i}
$$
Then we take the expectation of the estimate and put it in terms of the true first moment $E[g_t]$:
$$
\begin{align}
E[m_{t}]&=E\left[ (1-\beta_{1})\sum_{i=1}^t \beta_{1}^{t-i}g_{i} \right] \\
&= (1-\beta_{1})\sum_{i=1}^t \beta_{1}^{t-i}E[g_{i}] \\
&\approx E[g_{t}](1-\beta_{1})\sum_{i=1}^t \beta_{1}^{t-i} + \zeta \\
&= E[g_{t}](1-\beta_{1}^t)+\zeta 
\end{align}
$$
$$
\therefore E[g_{t}]=\frac{E[m_{t}]}{(1-\beta_{1}^t)}
$$
Then the bias corrected Adam algorithm is:
$$
\begin{align}
m_{t}&=\beta_{1}m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_{1})g_{t} \\ 
\hat{m_{t}}&=\frac{m_{t}}{(1-\beta_{1}^t)} \\
v_{t}&=\beta_{2}v_{t-1}+(1-\beta_{2})g_{t}^2 \\
\hat{v_{t}}&=\frac{v_{t}}{(1-\beta_{2}^t)} \\
W_{t}&=W_{t-1}-\eta\frac{\hat{m}_{t}}{\sqrt{ \hat{v}_{t} }+\epsilon}
\end{align}
$$
