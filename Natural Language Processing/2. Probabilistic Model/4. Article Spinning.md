# Problem Statement
In the early 2000s and 2010s blogging and other similar activities became increasingly more popular. Everyone wanted their own blogs to be popular and be read by as many viewers as possible. To do so people and companies opted to use Search Engine Optimization (SEO) to artificially increase their blog's ranks on search engines. This way readers had a higher chance to stumble onto their blog's when searching for things on Google and other platforms. One way of doing SEO is simply to release a lot of content. This however, is not an easy task as content creation takes time and effort. 

First of all, copying other people's articles was a strategy at some point, however, a very bad one at that as search engines could tell that they are duplicates and in fact would penalize those sites. One solution to this is to take an existing article and then replace enough of its words to make it sufficiently distinct while still making sense. This is article spinning. This could be done manually but ideally we want it automated. There are many ways of doing article spinning, but we will only discuss Markov Models.
# N-Gram Approach
In a generative model, our goal is to generate new words based on the previous ones. This is not what we want for article spinning, instead we wish to generate words based on the previous and next one, i.e. the middle words. So lets try to predict $p(s_t|s_{t-1}, s_{t+1})$. Using maximum likelihood estimation as before:
$$p(s_t|s_{t-1}, s_{t+1}) = \frac{\text{Count}(s_{t-1}\rightarrow s_t \rightarrow s_{t+1})}{\text{Count}(s_{t-1}\rightarrow ANY\rightarrow s_{t+1})}$$
Note that with article spinning, the goal is to obtain sensible replacements, the current model will give words that are likely to be appropriate to be in the middle of, however might change the meaning significantly. There is no POS tagging for the middle word, so instead of getting say a similar adjective for a word, it might give you a noun or something else entirely. This can be improved by taking into account POS.
