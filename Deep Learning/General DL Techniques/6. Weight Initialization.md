First we will showcase the most common sense approach to initialization and why this is not a good idea. In logistic regression, it is common to initialize the weights as $0$, so lets consider that for neural networks. Then in a neural network what happens if we initialize all the weights as $0$ (or a different constant)? Then we are treating every neuron of every layer identically, each neuron computes the same output and thus gets the same exact gradient at backpropagation. So each neuron in a layer updates identically to the rest of the neurons in the layer (symmetry). Remember that the goal of a neural network is to have the neurons learn different thing from the data, but by having identical weights, the neural network devolves into a single neuron with a single effective activation function (combination of the layers).

What about random initialization (sampling from a normal is most common, but uniform is also used)? If we have weights too large, it leads to exploding gradients, if too small then vanishing ones. The solution is to carefully scale the variance of the distribution we are sampling the weights from such that the variance of the weights and activations are roughly constant across the layers. In particular, We don't derive them explicitly though. 
# Xavier/Glorot Initialization
For the derivation see [Understanding the difficulty of training deep feedforward neural networks](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v9/glorot10a/glorot10a.pdf).
$$
W^{(l)}\sim U\left( -\sqrt{ \frac{6}{n^{(l-1)}+n^{(l)} }},\sqrt{ \frac{6}{n^{(l-1)}+n^{(l)} }} \right)
$$
Or alternatively:
$$
W^{(l)}\sim N\left( 0, \frac{2}{n^{(l-1)}+n^{(l)} } \right)
$$
Where $n^{(l)}$ is the number of neurons at layer $l$. $n^{(l-1)}$ is also known as the fan-in and $n^{(l)}$ as the fan out. Xavier initialization is mostly used for sigmoid and tanh activations as it was designed for such. We could also use LeCun initialization but this is an older and less performant method.
# Kaiming/He Initialization
For the derivation see [Delving Deep into Rectifiers: Surpassing Human-Level Performance on ImageNet Classification](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.01852).
$$
W^{(l)}\sim U\left( -\sqrt{ \frac{6}{n^{(l-1)} }},\sqrt{ \frac{6}{n^{(l-1)} }}\right)
$$
Or alternatively:
$$
W^{(l)}\sim N\left( 0,\frac{2}{ n^{(l-1)} } \right)
$$
Kaiming initialization is mostly used for ReLU activation and was devised due to Xavier working poorly with ReLU.
