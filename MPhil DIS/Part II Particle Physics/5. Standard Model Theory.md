The Standard model is a collection of related gauge theories which are quantum field theories that satisfy local gauge invariance:
- Electromagnetism: Quantum Electrodynamics (QED)
- Electroweak: Electroweak unification
- Strong: Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD)

To describe the fundamental interactions of particles we need a theory of relativistic quantum mechanics. We start from the Schrodinger equation for a free particle:
$$
\hat{E}\psi=\frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m}\psi  
$$
with energy and momentum operators $\hat{E}=i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}, \quad \hat{p}=-i \nabla$, which has plane wave solutions $\psi(\vec{r}, t)= Ne^{-i(Et-\vec{p} \cdot r)}$. This solution is 1st order in time and 2nd in space derivatives, so not Lorentz Invariant. What this tells us is that the Schrodinger equation cannot describe the physics of relativistic particles. Note how $E = p^2/2m$ is just the classical kinetic energy, plugging in their respective operators gives us the Schrodinger equation.
# Klein-Gordon Equation
To obtain a relativistic equation, we can use the energy relation from special relativity:
$$E^2=\vec{p}^2 + m^2$$
Then plugging in the wavefunction into this and substituting the energy and momentum operators in we obtain the Klein-Gordon Equation.
$$
-\frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial t^2}=-\nabla ^2\psi + m^2\psi
$$
$$
\frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial t^2}=(\nabla ^2 - m^2)\psi
$$
This gives us plane wave solutions but this time requiring $E^2=\vec{p}^2 + m^2$, allowing $E=\pm \sqrt{ \vec{p}^2 +m^2 }$, i.e. negative energy solutions are required to form a complete set of eigenstates. Thus implying the existence of Antimatter
