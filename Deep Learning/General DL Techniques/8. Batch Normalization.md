For more detail see [Batch Normalization: Accelerating Deep Network Training b y Reducing Internal Covariate Shift](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.03167).
Batch normalization is a technique used in neural networks to stabilize and accelerate training. Instead of only normalizing inputs as a preprocessing step, batch normalization normalizes the activations at each layer during training.

In practice, we train using mini-batches. For each batch, we compute the mean and variance of the activations and normalize them:
$$
\mu_B = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} x_i
$$
$$
\sigma_B^2 = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (x_i - \mu_B)^2
$$
$$
\hat{x}_i = \frac{x_i - \mu_B}{\sqrt{\sigma_B^2 + \epsilon}}
$$

To preserve the expressive power of the network, we then apply a learnable (updated via gradient descent) affine transformation:
$$y_i = \gamma \hat{x}_i + \beta$$
This allows the network to learn the optimal scale and shift for each feature.

Batch normalization is typically applied between the linear transformation and the activation function. Its goal is to reduce internal covariate shift as mentioned by the authors, which is to keep the feature distributions stable during training, thus making it less likely for the optimizer to get stuck in the saturated regime of the activation functions. I.e. keep the inputs within the dynamic range of the activations, similar to how we normally use normalization to keep inputs small, which in reality is done for this exact same reason.

At test time, we cannot rely on the mean and standard deviation of the test data, so instead we use the EWMA of the means and standard deviations during batch training. 

Overall batch normalization does 2 things. One, it allows us to increase the learning rate and thus increase training speed. And two, acts as a regularizer. Since inputs won't have extreme values due to the normalization and affine transformation, then the weights won't either. Final note, the bias term of the linear transformation is redundant with the one in the affine transformation, so it can and is completely discarded in implementations.
