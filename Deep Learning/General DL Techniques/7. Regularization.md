In Classical Machine Learning we use L2 regularization to encourage small weights and L1 to encourage sparsity in the weights. In neural networks we other specialized ways to regularize.
# Dropout
Taken and paraphrased from the abstract of [Dropout: A Simple Way to Prevent Neural Networks from Overfitting](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume15/srivastava14a/srivastava14a.pdf?utm_content=buffer79b4):
Deep neural nets with a large number of parameters are very powerful machine learning systems. However, overfitting is a serious problem in such networks. Large networks are also slow to use, making it difficult to deal with overfitting by use of ensemble methods. Dropout is a technique for addressing this problem. The key idea is to randomly drop units (along with their connections) from the neural network (at every layer including inputs) during training determined by some probability to keep or equivalently to not keep any specific unit. This prevents units from co-adapting too much. Instead of say 5 units learning a feature, it attempts to force less units to do the same, though at a larger scale. During training, dropout samples from an exponential number of different “thinned” networks by dropping units at each layer with some probability. At test time, all units are present but have their weights scaled by the probability to keep the unit at that layer. This approximates the averaging of the predictions done by the many thinned networks. This significantly reduces overfitting and gives major improvements over other regularization methods.
# Noise Injection
Consider a simple classification task between 2 classes. What would the decision boundary look like if we overfit a neural network to it? It would have very jagged lines. Then by introducing some random noise to the data (normal or uniform) we can help smoothen out the boundary decision. Another way of noise injection is to instead add noise to the weights, thereby making the model more robust to small changes in weights.
